## Draft 1: LinkedIn post (Aswin Nagappan)

> Most D2C brands overpay for mailer boxes for one reason: they reorder in small lots at a different spec each time.
>
> Three things that bring the unit cost down without making the box weaker:
> 1. Fix one or two standard sizes and design products around them.
> 2. Choose the board by test (burst, edge crush), not by "more ply = safer".
> 3. Order a quarter's volume in one run and approve a physical sample first.
>

## Draft 2: LinkedIn post (Periyanan)

> An export carton that fails in a container rarely fails because of the box alone. It fails because the box wasn't specified for stacking height, humidity and the journey.

> Before you send an export carton spec to any supplier, check: inner size vs product + fitment, ply and board grade for your stack height, shipping marks your buyer requires, and how many cartons fit per pallet and per 20 ft / 40 ft container.

## Draft 3: Quora answer ("What is the difference between 3-ply, 5-ply and 7-ply boxes?")

> Ply is the number of paper layers in the board. 3-ply has one wavy (fluted) layer between two flat liners; 5-ply has two fluted layers; 7-ply has three. More plies mean higher stacking strength and better protection, but also more weight and cost.
>
> Rough rule: 3-ply for light retail and e-commerce products, 5-ply for appliances and heavier shipping cartons, 7-ply for heavy, fragile or export loads. The paper grade (GSM and BF) matters as much as the ply count, so ask your supplier for both.
>
> (I work at a corrugated box manufacturer in Chennai. We wrote a longer explainer here: https://visalatchimanufacturing.com/blog/corrugated-box-ply-thickness-explained)
